# Slimebound Siege - Story Bible

This document is the narrative source of truth. For mechanics see
[GAME_DESIGN.md](GAME_DESIGN.md); for dialogue beats and format see
[DIALOGUE.md](DIALOGUE.md).

## Logline

A burned-out office worker dies, wakes up as the weakest monster in a fantasy war-game -
a slime - and discovers the only way home, or to freedom, is to out-think the kingdoms
that built this world to farm creatures like them.

## Premise

The protagonist is exhausted, ordinary, and from Earth. They die in a sudden traffic
accident while half-remembering a fantasy strategy game they played to unwind - one where
human towns defended against waves of monsters.

They wake in darkness: no arms, no legs, no human voice. A small slime in the wild
borderlands. A translucent, game-like **interface** appears - but it is broken,
incomplete, and worded as if the player is on the *enemy* side. Tutorials are missing.
Quest text is hostile. Achievements fire that were clearly never meant for a player.

The world names them a **horde core**: a rare monster that can guide lesser creatures
through instinct, mana, and absorbed memory. The nearest human village hunts monsters for
bounty. To survive the first night, the slime gathers a few weak creatures and sieges the
village before it can organize a purge.

That first siege teaches the truth of the world: it runs on **rules** - town defenses,
monster counters, unit roles, rewards, progression. If the slime does not grow, the
kingdoms will erase every monster nest on the frontier.

## Protagonist - The Slime ("Core")

- **What they are**: the player's avatar and strategic identity; a horde core. Mechanically
  the **Ace** card and the run's chosen **evolution core**.
- **Voice**: a wry, tired, deeply human inner monologue trapped in a body that gurgles. The
  comedy comes from a modern mind narrating medieval monster warfare. The pathos comes
  from fragments of a human life they can't fully recall.
- **Arc**: confusion and survival -> reluctant command -> deliberate strategy -> a leader
  who must decide what kind of power they want to be. The chosen evolution core
  (Commander / Absorber / Alchemist / Warden) is a narrative identity, not just a stat
  pack.
- **The Slime never dies to one bad assault.** It is the protected commander; losing it
  ends the *run*, framed as the horde core being driven back into dormancy.

## Narrative Device - The Broken Game UI

The diegetic interface is the story's signature voice and its central mystery.

- **Hostile framing**: quests phrased from the defenders' side ("Repel the infestation"),
  rewards labeled like loot tables, your monsters listed as "hostile entities."
- **Glitches as jokes**: achievements like *"First Blood (you weren't supposed to unlock
  this)"*, tooltips that argue with you, patch-note-style world events
  ("HOTFIX: militia morale increased after recent... incidents").
- **Glitches as mystery**: the UI knows things it shouldn't. It occasionally addresses the
  player, not the slime. It is the first clue that the world is *engineered* - leading to
  the Crown Engine.

The UI's comedy carries Act I; its mystery carries Act III.

## Themes

- **Identity and memory**: are you still the person who died? Does it matter?
- **Found family**: a horde is not an army of pawns; it becomes a people.
- **Dehumanization, literally**: the kingdoms insist monsters are soulless resources. The
  slime's retained humanity is the counter-argument.
- **The ethics of conquest**: you *are* the invader. The game asks what you do with power.
- **Free will vs the "game rules"**: who wrote this world, and can the rules be broken?

## Structure - Three Acts Over Five Kingdoms

The run's regions (antes) carry the acts. Story beats trigger on conquest milestones and
at Event nodes.

### Act I - Awakening (Frontier Farmlands)

Tone: comedy-forward, survival stakes.

- Wake as a slime; learn the world through the broken UI.
- The first desperate siege of a bounty-hunting village.
- Gather the first companions from the borderland nests.
- **First human defector** joins - a frightened militia conscript or a disgraced ex-guard
  who realizes the bounty system is a meat grinder.
- Beat: the slime chooses, for the first time, to protect the horde rather than just
  survive.

### Act II - The Horde (Iron Marches + Sun Dominion)

Tone: the balance tips toward stakes; the world pushes back.

- **Iron Marches**: the kingdom stops ignoring the frontier. A **Knight-Commander** is
  dispatched to hunt the horde core. Real military resistance; the found family is tested.
- **Sun Dominion**: religious anti-monster zealotry - "monsters have no souls." The
  **Inquisitor** weaponizes Holy Wards against your Slimes. The slime's human memories
  directly contradict the doctrine, and a Dominion citizen begins to doubt.
- **Permadeath bites here**: a named companion can fall for good. The story acknowledges
  the loss in later scenes; the survivors change.
- Beat: the horde is no longer a band of strays - it is a cause, and the kingdoms know it.

### Act III - The Engine (Frost Holds + Crown Engine Capital)

Tone: serious; the mystery pays off.

- **Frost Holds**: a cold, methodical region of slow-fields and ice walls; the last buffer
  before the capital. The **Frost Warden** fights a war of attrition.
- **Crown Engine Capital**: the truth. The **Crown Engine** is the machine that enforces
  the world's rules - spawning monsters to be farmed, reincarnating the dead, maintaining
  the broken "game." It is tied to *why the slime is here at all*.
- The **Architect** of the Engine is the final antagonist: not a cackling villain, but
  someone who believes the cycle is mercy.
- Climax: assault the capital and confront the Architect and the Engine itself.

## Character Roster

### Companions (named, permadeath-capable - your Horde)

- **A loyal goblin** (Clubs): the first recruit; comic, brave, in over its head. The
  heart of the early game.
- **A stoic brute** (Spades): an ogre/orc of few words and enormous patience; the
  found-family's quiet anchor.
- **A mirror slime-familiar** (Hearts): a smaller slime that echoes the protagonist - a
  way to externalize the slime's inner debate about what they're becoming.
- **A trickster caster** (Diamonds): an imp/shaman who treats the broken UI as a toy and
  pokes at the world's rules - thematically tied to Act III.

### Human Allies (defectors)

- **A militia deserter**: Act I; the first proof that "human" and "enemy" are not the same
  word.
- **A disgraced kingdom mage**: mid-game; knows how Wards and the Engine's lesser machines
  work; bridges the player toward the Act III revelation.

### Antagonists

- **The Knight-Commander** (Act II): a competent, honorable hunter who genuinely believes
  he protects people. The "enemy who isn't wrong."
- **The Inquisitor** (Act II): zealot; the soulless-monster doctrine given a face.
- **The Frost Warden** (Act III): cold attritional general; respects a worthy siege.
- **The Architect** (finale): builder of the Crown Engine; believes the cycle is mercy.

## Endings

Endings branch on accumulated ally choices (Event nodes) and how the player treated
conquered kingdoms.

- **Conqueror**: raze the kingdoms; monster supremacy. The horde wins, but the slime risks
  becoming the thing it fought.
- **Coexistence**: with enough human allies, broker a new order between monsters and
  people - the found family writ large.
- **Break the Engine**: destroy or seize the Crown Engine, ending the cycle - escape the
  isekai, or become the world's new core. The "answer the mystery" ending.

## Gameplay - Story Integration

- **Beats trigger on conquest** (clearing a town/capital) and at **Event nodes** on the run
  map.
- **Permadeath rewrites scenes**: a companion who dies is referenced as dead afterward;
  their horde buff is gone and the survivors' lines change.
- **The chosen evolution core is characterization**: Commander, Absorber, Alchemist, and
  Warden cores each color how the slime speaks and what endings come naturally.
- **The broken UI is the narrator**: most exposition, comedy, and the central mystery are
  delivered through interface text, not cutscenes - cheap to produce and on-theme.
- **Permanent conquest = persistent world**: kingdoms you break stay broken into later
  runs (meta-progression), so the story of the world advances across runs, not just within
  one.

## Open Story Questions (decide before production)

- Does "Break the Engine" literally return the protagonist to Earth, or is that a false
  hope the game deliberately complicates?
- How much do meta-progression unlocks reveal story between runs vs. within a single run?
- Do companions have individual recruitment quests, or do they arrive through Events?
